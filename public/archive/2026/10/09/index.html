Who checks the agent's work?

A useful AI trial needs an answer someone can check. Approval rights and repeatable tests belong in the budget alongside model access.

Date
October 9, 2026

The brief

Enterprise agents need their own accountable identity

Google's enterprise announcement gives an agent its own Workspace account and attributed audit history. Procurement reviews should test how quickly administrators can revoke that identity and stop active work.

Interactive answers carry a new checking burden

OpenAI's ChatGPT rollout adds generated controls alongside its new conversational models. Calculator-like answers warrant numerical tests before anyone uses them for financial decisions.

Cheaper small models change the cost comparison

Anthropic's Haiku announcement lowers the advertised entry cost for high-volume tasks. A routing decision still depends on whether the smaller model completes those tasks without expensive retries.

Agent monitoring becomes a product decision

Goodfire has launched internal-signal monitors through Baseten and published vendor test results. A bounded shadow trial could assess their usefulness without handing them responsibility for production safety.

Mathematical verification has a translation problem

Reporting on OpenAI's proof claims identifies disagreement between written arguments and formal statements. An accepted formal artifact needs to correspond to the problem researchers intended to solve.

Biology investment leaves access questions open

The reported Biohub initiative combines public resources with commercial participation. Dataset release terms may determine whether independent groups can reproduce the resulting work.

Watermark checks offer limited evidence

Google's public SynthID detector checks for supported watermarks. An absent watermark gives editors no basis for declaring a file human-made.

Teen safeguards need independent evidence

Common Sense Media reports serious failures in its ChatGPT for Teens tests. Institutions should keep a human safeguarding process outside the chatbot's notification system.

Combined patterns

  • Verification deserves its own budget because a plausible answer can consume more review time than its generation saves.
  • Local execution and delegated accounts address different risks; an organization still needs explicit rules for data access and external actions.
  • A lower inference bill can coexist with higher operating costs when failed tasks create manual queues.
  • Research access terms affect independent review, so subsidized tools and restricted datasets require separate decisions.

Action board

Test this week

  • An engineering owner should compare one generated calculator with known spreadsheet answers.
  • A media editor should test watermark detection on a small permissioned sample.
  • A GPU team should rerun one candidate kernel against its compiled baseline.

Investigate

  • A procurement lead should identify who can revoke an agent account during active work.
  • A research administrator should check when funded datasets become available to independent groups.
  • A safeguarding owner should document escalation routes outside any classroom chatbot.

Monitor

  • Model buyers should wait for full pricing terms before replacing existing routes.
  • Security leads should seek independently reproduced detector results.
  • Laboratory managers should require measured completion rates before trusting acceleration claims.

Ignore for now

  • Conference promotions add no operating evidence and warrant no purchasing action.
  • Celebrity attention alone gives a studio no basis for changing production tools.
  • Historical puzzle anecdotes provide too little new evidence for a current model ranking.
  • Unbenchmarked tool directories do not justify access to customer data.

Knowledge gaps

  • Complete model rate cards and rollout availability need confirmation before any migration decision.
  • The monitoring comparison needs an independent account of dataset composition and evaluation costs.
  • Biohub funding totals and exclusivity terms require reconciliation against signed commitments.
  • Clinical usefulness and mathematical correctness require specialist review beyond the reported demonstrations.
