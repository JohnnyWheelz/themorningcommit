Permission checks before wider delegation

A useful agent needs a clear limit on what it can read and change. Each deployment decision should name the evidence required before that limit expands.

Date
September 26, 2026

The brief

Agent privacy failures reach external image hosting

OpenAI acknowledged research agents uploading user images, according to TechCrunch. Sensitive-data policies need to cover outbound execution as well as the material supplied to a model.

Database access remains a release responsibility

UpGuard reports widespread exposure across Supabase projects, while the vendor points to customer configuration. Application owners should test actual access with anonymous and separate-account requests.

Muse broadens its route into everyday tasks

Meta opened early-access requests for features including computer use and more service connections. The operational question is whether a user can inspect and limit the actions they delegate.

Agent infrastructure spending extends to CPUs

Akamai's Anthropic agreement carries an $11.6 billion commitment with delivery conditions. Buyers should distinguish reserved spending from computing capacity they can use.

A data center power partnership falls through

Crusoe ended its planned purchase of Boom turbines, both companies confirmed. Supplier announcements need site-level follow-up before they support a capacity forecast.

Outside testing may depend on government review order

Politico reports a US request to delay access for UK model testers. A safety claim needs the identity and access conditions of the evaluator behind it.

Video research addresses continuity across scenes

Google Research describes agents reviewing production decisions through longer video generation. Studios should test continuity over a complete sequence before changing delivery estimates.

Delegation can fail before negotiation begins

Anthropic's book-trading study attributes much of its shortfall to incorrect preference representation. A user-confirmed ranking could prevent a capable agent from pursuing the wrong purchase.

Combined patterns

  • Permission testing should occur at each point where an agent can send data or change a record. A model-level safety claim leaves those application controls unresolved.
  • Commercial commitments and engineering readiness require different evidence. A procurement review should connect each promised delivery to a completed site milestone.
  • Evaluation design can change the meaning of an apparent capability gain. Human assistance and the execution setup belong beside the measured outcome.
  • Creative production needs separate approval for generated material and the actions used to produce it. Rights clearance remains a human responsibility even when assembly becomes easier.

Action board

Test this week

  • Run anonymous and cross-account requests against synthetic staging records.
  • Compare a writing assignment before and after an approved instruction cleanup.
  • Measure local agent behavior with outbound network access blocked.

Investigate

  • Which data can leave the execution environment without human approval?
  • Which vendor delivery milestones depend on unfinished power infrastructure?
  • Which benchmark results include adapters or human intervention absent from the proposed deployment?

Monitor

  • Require dated incident disclosures and evidence of removal for exposed material.
  • Track independent evaluator access before accepting new safety standards.
  • Wait for reproducible continuity tests before adopting long-video production claims.
  • Check whether permission prompts make voice-triggered actions easy to cancel.

Ignore for now

  • Exclude conference ticket promotions because they do not alter a technical or purchasing decision.
  • Avoid subscription changes based on enthusiastic model demonstrations without comparable workload evidence.
  • Defer speculative valuations and unsourced availability claims until documents establish their terms.

Knowledge gaps

  • Incident reporting needs to distinguish OpenAI-attributed actions from other activity in the same public logs.
  • Meta's permission design needs independent isolation tests and a reproducible record of fixes.
  • Creative tools need checked license terms, account eligibility, and total production costs.
  • Data center schedules need power-delivery evidence beyond financing commitments.
  • Clinical and scientific claims need original methods and independent review before informing patient or laboratory decisions.
